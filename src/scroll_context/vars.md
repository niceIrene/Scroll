# Your variables ARE your working context

In this configuration old steps leave this prompt aggressively, and your Python variables are what persists. Manage your context by managing your variables.

- **Tool output is transient.** A tool result appears in this prompt verbatim ONCE — the step it lands. After that it is replaced by an event log of the step: an `ops:` line (what was retrieved — op, hit counts, snippets vs full text; never the queries themselves, the step's `seq` recovers those verbatim), the names of variables created/changed, and a `⚠` line if a retrieval mostly re-fetched rows you already held (`↳` marks the intended snippet→full-text upgrade instead). Variable METADATA — schema, coverage, origin — lives in the `[working memory]` digest, always current. If you did not store what you found in a variable, the change log will say so — and you will have to re-derive it.
- **Your old reasoning is distilled.** Steps older than the last 2 keep only their `⟦ … ⟧` headline line (or, if you wrote none, a sentence-clipped summary of its forward-looking thought — so write ⟦headlines⟧ for anything a summary might miss). Anything you will need again — a value, a date, a decision, a dead end to skip — record it in a headline or a variable, never only in prose.
- **Closed turns shrink to records.** When a turn ends, its scaffolding leaves this prompt; what remains durable is a `kind='turn_record'` row (`Q: <ask> / A: <answer>`, the turn's seq span in metadata) — and at session end a `kind='session_record'` row — searchable anchors to your own past turns (`ms.search("…", kind='turn_record', scope='task')`, or the map's `T<n>` lines where the map is present).
- **The `[working memory]` digest each step lists every variable**: type, shape, size, what its rows COVER (sessions, dates, seq span), how it was obtained (op, count, snippets vs full text, and `seq N` — the producing step, whose durable row holds the exact query in `tool_input` and the raw output; `ms.expand([N])` recovers both), and your notes. Variables are listed in creation order; a line without a `seq N` belongs to the same step as the line above it (that step's distilled ⟦…⟧ line in the transcript says what it was doing). Rows tagged `snippets` are lossy previews — expand their seqs before quoting or substring-testing them. Small collections show their rows; large ones show metadata only. **Check a variable's coverage before issuing a new query** — if the sessions/dates you need are already covered, slice that variable in code instead of re-retrieving. A `⚠ … seqs already in \`X\`` flag means your latest retrieval mostly duplicated `X`: stop re-querying and work with what you have.
- **Name variables for what they hold** — `budget_rows`, `spa_mentions`, `anchors` — not `r2` or `tmp`. Single-letter loop temporaries are collapsed into one `(scratch: …)` line and carry no metadata, so anything you intend to reuse needs a real name.
- Curation ops available inside `{repl}`:
  - `note(name, text)` — attach a one-line description so future steps know what a variable means and why it matters.
  - `pin(name, level, note=...)` — keep a standing data view in every digest: `'meta'`, `'head'` (first rows), or `'full'` (capped). Pin the few variables holding your ANSWER-CRITICAL facts.
  - `show(x, n)` — print a bounded preview of a variable or value without dumping it into the window.
  - `del name` — drop a variable you no longer need; it leaves the digest (its history stays in the change log).

Work in this shape: **retrieve → reduce into a well-named variable (with a note) → answer from your variables.** When you find an anchor fact (a date, a number, a quote, a seq), store it immediately — e.g. `anchors = [{"event": "deploy prep", "date": "2025-03-01", "seq": 3791}]` then `note('anchors', 'candidate endpoints for the day-count')` — instead of trusting it to stay visible in the transcript.

**Store verdicts, not just finds — including negative ones.** A conclusion you reached is a fact worth keeping: when you establish that the specific asked-for fact is NOT in the history (only related discussion is), record it durably — e.g. `verdicts = {"samuel_advice": "not stated — only the user's own reference (checked S31-S40)"}` with a `note`, or a `⟦ not found: … ⟧` headline. Before answering, reconcile with your stored verdicts: topically-related material re-found at the last step does not override a recorded absence.
